# Product Graph input for a landing page

Product Graph is an optional research source. This plugin works from a plain business brief too. Read only the packets/paths the project supplies; do not require a room graph, registry, private checkout or particular directory layout.

Translate the available research into a small page brief:

| Research input | Landing-page decision |
|---|---|
| People, buyer language, demographics and situations | Primary audience, recognisable problem and vocabulary. |
| Demand, alternatives, competition and advertising | Positioning, differentiation, objections and channel context. |
| Studies, academic papers, trends and public figures | Supporting context and claims that the evidence actually permits. |
| Candidate angles and products | Chosen promise/offer and a reason to prefer it over alternatives. |
| Business cases, costs, suppliers and logistics | Feasibility and strategy; economics belong in the business-case comparison. |
| Research/product/supplier/logistics iteration | Explain how the business improves a case and makes a decision. |

An unselected set of angles is not an approved offer. Compare the few credible candidates on audience need, differentiation, evidence, operational feasibility and economics; recommend a direction with its assumptions. Obtain the missing business decision before presenting it as the selected public positioning. Do not silently pick the most exciting headline and retrofit the evidence.

Retain source links and provenance for factual claims. Public percentages need measured support or an explicit illustrative label. Influencer or famous-person coverage does not establish endorsement; an academic paper does not automatically establish a product outcome.

Keep internal research terminology out of public copy when it does not help the customer. Translate process labels into business language, concrete decisions and useful outputs. The person reviewing the page should understand what the product does, who benefits and what to do next.

Example input: research identifies three buyer groups, 22 angles and four supplier options. The next output is a chosen buyer/offer and a short evidence-backed brief. A finished page, invented conversion rate and copied private research database are not implied.
